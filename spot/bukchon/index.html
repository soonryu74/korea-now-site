When to go

There is no weekly closing day here — you can come any day of the week.

Counting foreign visitors to Jongno-gu, Seoul over twelve weeks, Tuesday is the quietest day you can actually go and Saturday the busiest — a difference of about 30%. These are district-wide figures rather than a headcount at the gate, so treat them as a tendency.

Opening hours are 10:00 to 17:00. Residential "quiet zone" alleys open to visitors 10:00–17:00 only. Tour groups tend to arrive mid-morning and again after lunch; the first hour after opening is normally the quietest stretch of any day.

What it costs, and what to bring

Entry is free. Nothing needs to be booked or queued for in advance.

Cards are accepted. Foreign Visa and Mastercard work almost everywhere in Korea, and contactless is normal.

English signage is partial — enough to find your way, not enough to explain what you are looking at. The Korean name above is worth showing to a taxi driver or a passer-by; it works better than pronouncing it.

Getting there

The nearest subway stop is Anguk (안국역) on Line 3, about 6 minutes on foot. Seoul's subway signs and announcements are in English, and a T-money card bought at any convenience store works on every line and bus.

Eat nearby

Seochon Village is 1.0 km away. Tongin Market, at the edge of Seochon, is where office workers and locals buy lunch one spoonful at a time.

  • Coin lunchbox 엽전 도시락 ₩5,000–10,000
  • Oil tteokbokki 기름떡볶이 ₩4,000–6,000

Gwangjang Market is 2.0 km away. The oldest market food alley in Seoul, and still the busiest. Everything is cooked in front of you.

  • Mung bean pancake 빈대떡 ₩5,000–8,000
  • Mini seaweed rolls 마약김밥 ₩3,000–5,000
  • Raw beef 육회 ₩15,000–25,000

Prices are per person and were checked in September 2026. Market stalls are often cash-only even where restaurants take cards.
