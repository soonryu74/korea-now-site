When to go

There is no weekly closing day here — you can come any day of the week.

Counting foreign visitors to Jung-gu, Busan over twelve weeks, Wednesday is the quietest day you can actually go and Sunday the busiest — a difference of about 15%. These are district-wide figures rather than a headcount at the gate, so treat them as a tendency.

Opening hours are 05:00 to 22:00. It stays open into the evening, which is usually the calmest and coolest time to come. Tour groups tend to arrive mid-morning and again after lunch; the first hour after opening is normally the quietest stretch of any day.

What it costs, and what to bring

Entry is free. Nothing needs to be booked or queued for in advance.

Cards are accepted. Foreign Visa and Mastercard work almost everywhere in Korea, and contactless is normal.

English signage is partial — enough to find your way, not enough to explain what you are looking at. The Korean name above is worth showing to a taxi driver or a passer-by; it works better than pronouncing it.

Getting there

There is no subway station close by. A taxi or an intercity bus is usually the way in — show the Korean name above to the driver.

Eat nearby

Bupyeong Kkangtong Night Market is 533 m away. A night market that only starts after dark. Small plates, low prices, no menus in English.

Prices are per person and were checked in September 2026. Market stalls are often cash-only even where restaurants take cards.
